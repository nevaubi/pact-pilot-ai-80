# Practice Partner AI

Attached is a zip of a platform my colleague created, the goal is for me to recreate it now for users with more modern code and ai capabilities but not stray too far from the ui ux and concept. I’ll give you the big message he gave me, use it and build. use lovable ai as applicable, lovable cloud. 



His message:



<text>



Let’s work together to refine and structure a well-throughout platform for transactional and compliance law firms, and later upgrade it for different types of practices. Below are my brainstorm thoughts, and attached is the code file for Mirza.

Perspectives:

As discussed, the goal here is for the platform (Mirza) to be tailored to small to mid-size (let’s say less than 50 attorneys) boutique law firms. The client base of these firms are generally small to middle market, and generally private organizations. It’s not tailored to law firms representing public companies, complex litigation, or institutional organizations. This is an important perspective because it allows us to filter each workflow and action in Mirza, and the UI and usability, the AI capabilities, and who the target customers are. These firms would also pay a bit of premium if the software is tailored to them and right sized, vs. buying general use platforms (Clio, etc.) or an overly complex or capable platform.

My reading of the room from professional firms (remember, small to mid-size) has been that they don’t have the capital to do mass migrations of platforms, data, or have enough manpower to address downtime issues, etc. Thus, it’s critical that the platform is private, secure, cost effective, and efficient in all aspects, and the data structure one that allows for portability and standard backup systems, and using/migrating to the system is not a big project. Based on my understanding, they want less dependency on too many components – which means that the chance of something going wrong gets higher. So the more self-sustained system, the better.

On the AI capability, the challenge has been that it’s evolving too much and can do too much – it’s a genie in a bottle, and sometimes users freeze because it can do so much. The way to control this is to find a good balance where the AI capabilities do what associates and staff would do, but not overwhelm the partner. The partner is using the AI, and the AI capabilities would be equivalent to having 5 associates and 5 staff. I can explain this more, but I’m trying impress a perspective.

For example, and this is just an idea, it would be great if the AI capabilities had an Advanced vs Normal AI Effort models. The Normal would be for basic processing/work (no research, just case management and drafting, etc). The Advanced would be for more advanced tasks. This is a clear demarcation for the user on what they are working on. This would also reduce – presumably – the AI token usage, which means the pricing could be less to attract more customers.

Cost and token usage is another big concern for small to midsize firms. They don’t have clients that have deep pockets, and clients do review bills before they pay. If the capabilities cost a lot in tokens, they are less likely to tie themselves to 

Don’t baby – or stunt growth – of professionals. Firms have a hierarchy of people that grow in their carrier. If the AI capabilities take away from skills, most firms are opposed to this. The AI is supposed to be support, not the primary performer. The AI capabilities should train and support the user, but prompt the user to review and absorb what is created.

The user experience and UI/UX should be intuitive, the platform usability easy as one wants it, and as complex as one wants it. I think you are starting to get the message.

Mirza Plaform Core Capabilities.

Based on all of the above, here are few additional capabilities that Mirza can have that would be awesome: 

Meeting Note Taker – a button on a matter.

AI “Mic” or “Button” on each matter, OR, launch AI workspace for the entire firm. Remember that in professional firms, people mostly work on active matters – so it’s good to keep their focus on what they need to work on, rather than starting globally on the platform. This is one of the issues I see with current platforms, they are “global” AI notifications and prompts to help, not focused on matters. Also, they launch on their own, rather than being prompted.

File processing. (Deal documents come in, LOI comes in, for estate planning, the Questionnaire comes in). The idea here is a user can dump transaction documents for a deal, and the Matter would map out everything, including dates, deadlines, deliverables, etc. The key here is to no over-process the document, but to create a structured process to get the deal through.

Integration with email and calendar – self explanatory.

Full MS Office and Acrobat type apps. Later allow for collaboration between Firm members, and e-signature. We spoke about this, it would be cool if users could truly have everything they need in one system. This would reduce the need for getting more subscriptions or software.

Anything else you can think of would be great.

The above are just more features on Mirza. And think of it that it should be an awesome program to use even without the AI capabilities. You don’t want to lose the usability and core capabilities of Mirza in return for better AI capabilities. The AI capabilities are supporting/on top of what Mirza does already.

AI Capabilities.

Drafting Assistant. This would be a state of the art drafting assistant, especially if it’s part of the Office and PDF suite, and tied to Mirza. Later on, it can generate and update documents based on list of questions, or just explanations. The key here is how you keep documents close to “house templates”. Attorneys don’t like having different templates every deal – they want standardized documents that they are familiar with, and clauses provisions that they are familiar with. If the drafting assistant creates different documents every time, this creates a heavy burden of the attorney reviewing different documents every single time. So the forms should default to house templates. 

Closing Assistant. Closing is a big deal in transactional work. It’s about creating a checklist, who does what, where deliverables stand, prepping for closing, and closing the deal. The practice has been for firms to create custom “closing checklists” for deals, the parties manually update that list and follow the deliverables. Let’s see what AI can do to make this process smoother.

Contract Processing and Management. This is about when the firm receives a new deal with draft – or sometimes executed – contracts, and the firm must help process those contracts. The firm also “manages” the contract: when is the term and termination, what are the insurance requirement, etc.

Compliance (corporate entities, trusts, etc.). This is more about governmental filings, and compliance with contract or other obligations. Sometimes tied to Contract processing and Management, but no always. For entities, it’s keeping track of what filings need to be made, to check UCC filings, etc. Sometimes it about generating notices per contracts/documents, etc.

Case Law Research (Illinois and all Federal Law). The platform should always vet even normal transactions against current case law, but most transactional attorneys don’t use these that often. US law is very flexible on contracts and trusts, so unless there is a specific prohibition on something, everything else is allowed. It would be great to have a standard case law and regulations/statues research platform, but in transactional practice, it’s more about “compliance”, rather than case law research. 

Estate Planning. This is a very easy one. Generally, attorneys sent questionnaires, client filles it out, then attorneys talk about what the goals of the client are, and once that is done, drafting begins. I already have a set of documents that I created for various documents and estate planning documents that can be used here.

Tax Compliance/Checker. This would be super awesome. The idea here is for transactions (M&A, finance, investments) to be put through a tax analysis, and give the user a background of the tax code provisions in play. While lawyers that are NOT CPAs are not allowed to advise on tax, it’s good for the attorneys to at least know what potential red flags are there with tax implications.

International Compliance (cross-border transactions and compliance, choose between two jurisdictions).

Finance (Market Rate, various industry grade financing documents). Aside from generating various finance documents (lending, equity, etc.), the other key aspect here is to vet – presumably through SEC – on what is the market on those terms. 

Real Estate (processing, title review, survey review, and closing transaction). This is one that can be automated, and if done correctly, it’s super useful. Every purchase and sale in Illinois has attorneys involved. In residential, it’s volume, and in commercial, it’s about processing the deal. Like most transactional work, it’s about contract, due indigence, financing, and closing.

Again, if someone gave me AI that does all of this – there would be two problems with those: (1) the AI is going to over-complicate what is customary; (2) every deal feels like a different transaction, even when it’s routine/common transaction. So even if the AI capabilities on the above go 60% of what AIs can do today, that would be enough for 99% of cases. It’s suppose to be supportive, not primary. Lawyers should not be following AI, the AI should be supporting the attorneys. So, figure out what kinds of things the AI capabilities can do that can be helpful, rather than it doing everything.



</text>
